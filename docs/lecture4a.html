Normalising flows

A flexible density from invertible maps, via the change-of-variables formula.

Why we need flows

Real posteriors in physics are often multimodal, curved, or heavy-tailed. Gaussians can't represent them.

① Evaluate \(\log q_\phi(\boldsymbol\theta\mid\mathbf{x})\)

Needed for the NPE loss.
② Sample \(\boldsymbol\theta \sim q_\phi\)

Needed for corner plots, downstream analyses.
③ Arbitrary shape

Multimodal, curved, heavy-tailed.

MLPs on raw outputs fail ①: nothing forces normalisation.
Normalising flows satisfy all three by construction, via the change-of-variables formula.

The big picture

A simple base density \(\pi_0(\mathbf{z}_0)\) is pushed through a chain of invertible maps \(f_i\) into a complex target \(\pi_K(\mathbf{z}_K)\).

Figure: Lilian Weng, “Flow-based Deep Generative Models” (2018), lilianweng.github.io, after Weng's adaptation of Rezende & Mohamed (2015), arXiv:1505.05770.

Change of variables

Normalising flows are based on the change-of-variables formula.

Univariate case. If \(z \sim p_Z(z)\) and \(\theta = f(z)\) with \(f\) invertible and differentiable, \[ p_\Theta(\theta) \;=\; p_Z(z)\,\left|\frac{dz}{d\theta}\right| \;=\; p_Z(f^{-1}(\theta))\,\left|\frac{df}{dz}\right|^{-1} \]
Multivariate case. For \(\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^d\), and a mapping \(\boldsymbol\theta = f(\mathbf{z})\), \[ p_\Theta(\boldsymbol\theta) \;=\; p_Z(f^{-1}(\boldsymbol\theta))\;\left|\det \frac{\partial f}{\partial \mathbf{z}}\right|^{-1} \] The Jacobian determinant is the volume rescaling. General \(|\det J|\) costs \(\mathcal{O}(d^3)\).

Example: warping a Gaussian

The volume compression can be nicely visualised in the univariate case.

map  \(\theta = z + A\sin z\)
base  \(\mathcal{N}(0, 1.5^2)\)
warped  \(p_\Theta(\theta) = p_Z(z)/|1+A\cos z|\)
monotonic ✓

Base \(\mathcal{N}(0,1.5^2)\) pushed through \(\theta = z + A\sin z\). For \(A < 1\) the map is strictly monotonic, still invertible, but as \(A\to 1\) the derivative near \(z=\pm\pi\) collapses, so density mass piles up there: the warped target becomes bimodal.

Normalising flow = stack of invertible maps

to build a normalising flow, we now stack many simple invertible maps to generate a complex one.

\[ \boldsymbol\theta \;=\; f_L \circ f_{L-1} \circ \cdots \circ f_1(\mathbf{z}), \qquad \mathbf{z} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d) \] Each \(f_\ell\) is invertible and differentiable, with parameters \(\phi_\ell\) (possibly conditioned on \(\mathbf{x}\)).
  • Sampling \(\boldsymbol\theta \sim q_\phi\): draw \(\mathbf{z}\), push forward through the stack.
  • Density evaluation at a given \(\boldsymbol\theta\): invert the stack, evaluate \(p_Z\), correct with Jacobians.
  • Composition of invertible maps is invertible, deep flows stay a valid density.
Flexibility grows with depth \(L\), just like MLPs, but every layer preserves the probability axioms.

Log-density by chain of Jacobians

Evaluating the loss function (NLL) is formally straightforward.

Let \(\mathbf{z}_\ell = f_\ell(\mathbf{z}_{\ell-1})\), \(\mathbf{z}_0 = \mathbf{z}\), \(\mathbf{z}_L = \boldsymbol\theta\). Change-of-variables composes: \[ \log q_\phi(\boldsymbol\theta\mid\mathbf{x}) \;=\; \log p_Z(\mathbf{z}_0) \;-\; \sum_{\ell=1}^{L} \log \left| \det \frac{\partial f_\ell}{\partial \mathbf{z}_{\ell-1}} \right| \]
Training is just minibatch SGD: \[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NPE}}(\phi) = -\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\sum_{(\boldsymbol\theta, \mathbf{x})\in\mathcal{B}} \log q_\phi(\boldsymbol\theta\mid\mathbf{x}) \]

Key design constraint: each \(|\det \partial f_\ell / \partial \mathbf{z}_{\ell-1}|\) must be computationally cheap.

Example: Affine coupling layers

A specific type of mapping with cheap log determinant are affine coupling layers.
Idea: For each mapping, split parameters vector in two components \(\mathbf{z} = (\mathbf{z}_A, \mathbf{z}_B)\). Define the transformation as \[ \mathbf{z}'_A = \mathbf{z}_A, \qquad \mathbf{z}'_B = \mathbf{z}_B \odot \exp\!\big(s_\phi(\mathbf{z}_A)\big) \;+\; t_\phi(\mathbf{z}_A) \] where \(s_\phi, t_\phi\) are arbitrary neural networks (MLPs).
Invertibility
\(\mathbf{z}_A\) passes through untouched; \(\mathbf{z}_B\) is scaled-and-shifted given \(\mathbf{z}_A\). Trivially invertible in closed form.
Cheap Jacobian
\(J\) is triangular ⇒ \(\log|\det J| = \sum_i s_\phi(\mathbf{z}_A)_i\). \(\mathcal{O}(d)\), not \(\mathcal{O}(d^3)\).

Then alternate which half is "active" across layer. Stack \(L\) of these → expressive flow.

Conditional flows, plugging in the data

For SBI we want \(q_\phi(\boldsymbol\theta \mid \mathbf{x})\), not just \(q_\phi(\boldsymbol\theta)\).
Let the scale and shift networks depend on the data (or its embedding): \[ s_\phi(\mathbf{z}_A, T_\phi(\mathbf{x})), \qquad t_\phi(\mathbf{z}_A, T_\phi(\mathbf{x})) \]
End-to-end picture.
  1. Embedding \(T_\phi(\mathbf{x})\) compresses the data (CNN / Transformer / GNN, see Lec 4).
  2. Conditional flow turns \(\mathbf{z}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)\) into \(\boldsymbol\theta\), with all coupling-layer parameters depending on \(T_\phi(\mathbf{x})\).
  3. Loss: \(-\log q_\phi(\boldsymbol\theta^\star\mid\mathbf{x})\) averaged over simulator pairs.
Every component, embedding and coupling networks \(s, t\), is trained together by backprop.

Base samples \(\mathbf{z}\sim\mathcal{N}(0, I)\) are warped layer-by-layer into the target shape (here: two-moons). Each layer's Jacobian contributes to the log-density, keeping normalisation exact.

In the conditional version, the target shape depends on \(\mathbf{x}\), each observation gets its own warped posterior.

The full pipeline, revisited

Simulator: physics model, possibly slow.
Embedding \(T_\phi\): CNN / Transformer / GNN, matches the data modality.
Conditional flow: coupling layers conditioned on \(T_\phi(\mathbf{x})\).
Inference loss: \(-\log q_\phi(\boldsymbol\theta^\star\mid\mathbf{x})\).
Amortised: train once, then inference on any new \(\mathbf{x}_{\text{obs}}\) is a single forward pass + sampling.

Application, stochastic GW background with LISA

Posteriors for stochastic-background amplitude \(\alpha\), tilt \(\gamma\), and two noise parameters \(A\), \(P\). Inset: injected PSD, noise + SGWB + unresolved transients.
Problem. LISA data = instrument noise + a stochastic GW background + hundreds of unresolved transient sources (SMBH mergers). Goal: infer the background parameters.
Why SBI? The likelihood over the transient population is intractable. Traditional MCMC either ignores transients (biased, orange dashed) or has to sample their full parameter space (prohibitively expensive).
Result. The SBI posterior (orange solid, TMNRE via saqqara) automatically marginalises over the transients and recovers the true injection, matching MCMC (black) on the clean case C1.
Take-away. SBI turns a nuisance (the transient population) into something implicit in the simulator, no likelihood engineering needed.

Alvey, Bhardwaj, Domcke, Pieroni & Weniger, arXiv:2309.07954, code: saqqara.

Continuous-time heads

Flow matching and diffusion, two continuous-time pictures sharing one visual anchor, ODE vs SDE.

Continuous-time generative models, one picture

Coupling flows compose discrete invertible maps. The next two families take the same idea to continuous time: pull samples through a vector field over \(t\in[0, 1]\).

Flow matching, ODE

\(d\theta/dt = v_\phi(\theta, t)\), smooth, deterministic trajectories.

Diffusion, SDE

\(d\theta = b_\phi(\theta, t)\,dt + g(t)\,dW_t\), noisy, stochastic paths.

Both transport a base \(p_0\) (Gaussian noise) into the target \(p_1\) (posterior) over \(t\in[0,1]\). One uses an ODE, the other an SDE.

Flow matching

Learn a velocity field \(v_\phi(\theta, t)\) such that the ODE \(\;d\theta_t/dt = v_\phi(\theta_t, t)\), \(\theta_0 \sim p_0\), \(\theta_1 \sim p(\theta\mid x)\) transports the base noise to the posterior.

Training, conditional FM objective. Pick a "probability path" \(p_t(\theta\mid\theta_1)\) (e.g. straight-line \(\theta_t = (1-t)\theta_0 + t\theta_1\)); target velocity \(u_t(\theta\mid\theta_1)\). Regress: \[ \mathcal{L}_{\rm FM}(\phi) = \mathbb{E}_{t,\theta_0,\theta_1}\bigl\| v_\phi(\theta_t, t, x) - u_t(\theta\mid\theta_1)\bigr\|^2 \]

Sampling. Draw \(\theta_0\sim p_0\); integrate the learned ODE with any black-box solver (Euler, RK4, adaptive). Typically 10–50 steps.

Density. Available via instantaneous change-of-variables (Hutchinson trace), rarely cheap. FM is mostly used as a sampler.

Lipman et al. 2023 (ICLR). SBI: Dax et al. 2023 (DINGO-FM), Wildberger et al. 2023.

Diffusion / score-based generative models

Define a forward SDE that turns data into noise:

\( d\theta_t = -\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\theta_t\,dt + \sqrt{\beta(t)}\,dW_t, \quad t\in[0, 1] \)

Learn the score \(s_\phi(\theta, t, x) \approx \nabla_\theta\log p_t(\theta\mid x)\) by denoising:

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\rm DSM}(\phi) = \mathbb{E}_{t, \theta_0, x}\bigl\| s_\phi(\theta_t, t, x) - \nabla_\theta\log p_t(\theta_t\mid\theta_0)\bigr\|^2 \]

Sampling = integrate the reverse SDE (or its probability-flow ODE) from \(t=1\) (noise) back to \(t=0\) (sample). 50–500 steps typical.

SBI flavour. Condition the score on \(x\) (or \(T_\phi(x)\)). One trained score net amortises over all observations.

Sohl-Dickstein et al. 2015; Song & Ermon 2019; Ho et al. (DDPM) 2020; Song et al. (score SDE) 2021. SBI: Geffner et al. 2023, Sharrock et al. 2024.
